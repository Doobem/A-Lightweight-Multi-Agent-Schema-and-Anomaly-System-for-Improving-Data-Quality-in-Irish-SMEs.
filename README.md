# A-Lightweight-Agentic-Schema-and-Anomaly-Detection-System-for-Improving-Data-Quality-in-Irish-SMEs.
## Multi-Agent System

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) depend on data driven decision making. Despite that, their datasets often suffer from schema inconsistencies and irregularities that undermine analytical reliability. This paper addresses the problem of poor data quality in SMEs by investigating how effective a lightweight agentic AI system is at detecting schema inconsistencies and statistical anomalies, and to what extent such as system can enhance SME data. SMEs rarely have dedicated data teams; existing tools are too expensive and lack adaptability across diverse domains. A multi agent schema and anomaly detection system was developed and implemented across SME datasets. It demonstrates that a lightweight agentic system can perform effective anomaly detection across structurally different datasets without extensive configuration or domain specific turning. However, the system also exhibit limitation, particularly in domain interpretation and in distinguishing meaningful outliers from valid business events. These findings position the paper within the current state of art by showing that lightweight agentic system can match rule-based anomaly detectors but still fall short of contextual reasoning. SMEs can adopt low-cost agentic systems to automate data quality checks and improving downstream analytics. The challenge remain that the multi-agent lacks business logic and require human analysts to interpret flagged anomalies. Future work should focus on developing domain aware agents and human-in-the-loop frameworks that combine Artificial Intelligence (AI) with expert judgment.

## Introduction
Small and medium sized enterprises (SMEs) are the backbone of Irelands economy and represents most of the business across the European Union (European Commission, 2023). Despite their economic significance, SMEs continue to lag larger organisations in adopting advanced digital technologies, particularly artificial intelligence (AI). Existing enterprise solutions remain too costly, infrastructure intensive and cloud dependent for widespread SME adoption. Emerging technologies such as large language models (LLMs) and Agentic AI offer promising opportunities for automating data quality tasks. Recent research demonstrates that LLMs can support data wrangling, anomaly detection, and transformation code generation (Li and Döhmen, 2024). However, edge optimised AI models enable local deployment on low resource hardware (Cheng et al., 2025). However, most current implementations are cloud based, expensive and often incompatible with SMEs cost constraints, infrastructure limitations and GDPR related concerns.

The persistent misalignment between existing enterprise grade, cloud-based AI and data quality solutions are the challenging issues of Irish SMEs. Most enterprise AI tools assume access to substantial financial resources, higher digital infrastructure and specialist technical expertise, which many SMEs do not possess. This result to SMEs struggling to implement effective data quality processes. It undermines their ability to leverage analytics and AI for operational decision making (Kraus et al., 2021; Ben Slimane et al., 2022). This motivates the growing recognition that data quality is one of the most significant barriers to AI adoption in SMEs. Poor data quality such as missing values, inconsistent categories, duplicate records, and manual entry errors affects the accuracy of analytics and machine learning outputs (Rajan, 2024). SMEs experience these issues than larger organisations due to fragmented systems and the absence of formal data governance structures (Ben Slimane et al., 2022). This research aims to contributes to practical, scalable, and SME appropriate approach to AI driven data quality management.

The existing literature highlights a clear gap, although AI capabilities have advanced rapidly. Agentic AI frameworks have intercepted as an emerging solution which can transform how SMEs can handle operational, strategic and adaptive challenges (Olujimi et al., 2 025). There is currently no lightweight deployable Agentic system designed specifically to automate data quality tasks for SMEs using free, open-source tools. This has prompted the need to explore how a lightweight Agentic system can be designed and implemented to automatically improve data quality for SMEs in resource‑constrained environments. The primary research questions addressed in this study include:
•	How effective is a lightweight Agentic system in detecting schema inconsistencies and statistical anomalies in SME datasets?
•	What extent does an Agentic system enhance the accuracy, completeness and consistency of data used in SME?

To address these questions, this study proposes a lightweight agentic farmwork designed to improve data quality in Irish SMEs by automating schema validation and anomaly detection workflows. The system integrates a Schema Agent and Anomaly Agent; for structural and format validation and detecting missing values and numeric outliers respectively. This Agentic system forms a streamlined efficient pipeline suitable for SMEs that lack the resources to adopt complex enterprise grade data quality solutions. The contributions of this work are as follows:
1.	A lightweight two agent architecture that combines schema validation and statistical anomaly detection to improves spreadsheet data quality in Irish SMEs.
2.	Automated multi sheet reporting, enabling SMEs to receive clear, structured outputs containing schema inconsistencies and statistical anomalies.
3.	Seamless integration with SharePoint, allowing datasets to be automatically retrieved and processed withing existing SME workflows.

The major contribution of this research is the novel multi agent system capable of performing semantic schema inference and statistical anomaly detection using publicly available SME datasets. In addition, the use of a locally deployed large language model (LLM) and its GDPR compliance in demonstrating a practical method for SME to adopt AI without exposing data to external cloud services.
Minor contributions include the colour coded anomaly highlights in Excel, making results accessible to non-technical SME users. The integration of AI driven tools existing in Microsoft 365 workflows without major infrastructure changes.

This paper is organized as follows: the section of Literature Review examines recent research on SME data quality challenges, Multi Agent system and existing AI frameworks. The Methodology section outlines the design science approach used to develop and evaluate the Agentic system. The System Design section details the architectural decisions, agent responsibilities and workflow integration. The Implementation section describes the development of the Schema Agent, Anomaly Agent and automated reporting pipeline. The Evaluation section presents results from testing the system on SME datasets, including performance analysis and detection accuracy. Finally, the Conclusion summarizes the findings and provides recommendations for future works, including extensions into semantic validation and automated data cleaning.

###  Problem Identification
The research problem addressed in this study is practical and technical; SMEs in Ireland lack automated, affordable and intelligent tools for improving data quality. This aligns with DSR’s on solving real world problems through technological artefacts (Yigit Ozkan and Spruit, 2023). Poor data quality has been shown to negatively affect organisational performance, forecasting accuracy and compliance. Existing data quality tools are often too complex or too costly for SMEs (Scarton et al., 2025). This has created a need for lightweight and automated solutions. Furthermore, recent work on data quality emphasises that reporting anomalies significantly undermine analytical reliability and decision making (Fadlallah et al., 2023). This problem therefore highlights the need for this system, designing a lightweight automated multi agent detection system that performs schema validation and detects statistical anomalies. This aligns with recent research for accessible AI solutions tailored to SMEs (Ben Slimane et al., 2022).

###  Objective definition
A lightweight automated agent-based system is developed to address these problems faced by SMEs. The Schema agent interprets spreadsheet schemas using semantic inference, and validating schema consistency. The Anomaly agent aims to detects statistical anomalies and generating a structured report. This duo-agent is fully operating locally using Ollama to preserve data privacy. Integration into SME workflows is done seamlessly through SharePoint. These objectives align with accessible and low barrier AI tools or SMEs (Ben Slimane et al., 2022) (Eiffe et al., 2025).

###  Artefact Design and Development
Guided by the problem identified, the artefact was conceptualised as a Python based pipeline orchestrated through Lang Graph. The design centres on two specialised agents, Schema validation and anomaly detection. The Schema validation agent evaluates numeric completeness, data validity and type consistency to ensure data conforms to expected patterns. The anomaly agent applies lightweight profiling using the mean heuristic, an acceptable anomaly detection due to its simplicity and interpretability (Huang et al., 2025).  For semantic tasks, the system integrates Ollama for compact language models to run locally on hardware. Recent research demonstrates that Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively support data wrangling and semantic inference without requiring cloud infrastructure (Li and Döhmen, 2024; Cheng et al., 2025). The artefact integrates Microsoft Graph API to automate the ingestion and output of Excel files stored in SharePoint repertories. This ensures seamless alignment with SME friendly tools and environments. Integrating validation processes into existing enterprise ecosystems significantly improves adoption and operational reliability (Moreira et al., 2024).

This project uses publicly accessible datasets obtained from CSO, Data government portal and open data portals that publish SME data (CSO, 2026; Open Data Team, 2026). Public data portals increasingly serve as a critical infrastructure for evidenced based digital transformation (Fang et al., 2024). These datasets provide realistic examples of the types of tabular data commonly encountered in SMEs; such as financial records, operational data, and survey-based datasets. In the experimental phase, additional dataset was utilised to introduced common data quality issues. This approach aligns with established practices in data quality research, where controlled corruption enables reproducible evaluation of cleaning methods (Dawale et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025). 

###  Implementation
Transforming the conceptual duo-agent framework into fully operational system for analysing real SME dataset, Python was the development environment. This is due to its extensive ecosystem for data processing libraries and it sustainability for agent-based workflows. Lang Graph orchestration provided management and controlled message passing between agents. The Schema agent was implemented first using a locally deployed LLM through Ollama to infer data types, column, and semantic roles. Research show that the effectiveness of LLMs in semantic data interpretation, particularly when deployed locally to preserve privacy  (Rangineni, 2023; John M, 2025). Anomaly agent incorporates rule-based logic and statistical methods such as outlier detection and z-score standardization. Integration with SharePoint was achieved using Microsoft Graph API, enabling automated retrieval and upload of datasets. This reflects current SME digitalization trends, where cloud-based collaboration platforms are used to manage operational data.

###  Evaluation
Technical evaluation involved assessing the accuracy, robustness and consistency of the anomaly detection mechanisms. To ascertain accuracy, manual inspection of subset of anomalies flagged by the system and comparing them against known data quality issues. The system was evaluated across multiple datasets with varying structures, size and level of complexity. Functional evaluation focused on the usability and interpretability of the outputs. The excel highlighting feature was evaluated by examining whether the user’s ability to understand data issues without requiring technical expertise. This aligns with the recent research emphasising the importance of human centred design in AI driven data quality tools (Stefan et al., 2019).

###  Communication
In accordance with DSR principles; communication is essential for demonstrating the relevance and contribution of the artefact (Drechsler et al., 2022). The systems architecture, implementation and evaluation outcomes are documented comprehensively within the paper, ensuring transparency and reproducibility. The communication phase ensures that the artefact not only advances academic knowledge but also provides practical value to organisations seeking to improve their data quality practice.

###  System Architecture
The system is designed as a multi-layer architecture that processes publicly available SME related datasets. The given architecture aligns with Morden AI system engineering principles that emphasises modularity and explainability (Floridi and Cowls, 2021). It ingests SME data files, interprets the file using a local large language model (LLM) to detect anomalies using statistical and logical methods. This generates a tabular report and triggers automated notifications through Microsoft teams using Power Automate (figure 3). Each layer is designed to operate independently while contributing to a cohesive end to end workflow.

##  Conclusion and Future Work
This paper set out to evaluate the effectiveness of a lightweight agentic AI system in detecting schema inconsistencies and anomalies within SME datasets. In addition, accessing the extent to which such the system can enhance the accuracy, completeness and consistency of SME data. The multi agent system successfully identified a wide range of anomalies across different case studies; including missing values schema inconsistencies, and outliers (figure 4). The Schema inconsistencies were reliably detected, even in datasets with minimal structural variation (Figure 13). Statistical anomalies such as outliers and suspicious zeros were consistently flagged, demonstrating strong numerical sensitivity. The system struggles with domain specific interpretation; anomaly density varies significantly across SME datasets. Human analysts are required, as the multi agent system cannot distinguish between legitimate business events and erroneous anomalies. These findings confirm that while agentic systems can automate anomaly and schema detection, they cannot replace human expertise. The implications of this paper are significant for SMEs; they often lack dedicated data teams. It can reduce manual inspection time for SMEs and allow operational efficiency. Future research should develop agent that incorporates domain knowledge to reduce false positive and improve contextual accuracy. Design workflows should have human in the loop, where analysts guide, validate and refine agent decisions. This will yield hybrid intelligence system that combines automation with expert judgement. This research demonstrates that lightweight agentic system can significantly improve SME data quality by automating anomaly detection across diverse datasets. The paper holds a strong foundation for future research and commercial development in agentic data quality automation.
